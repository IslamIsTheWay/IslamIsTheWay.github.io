As-Sajdah 32:16 — explained simplyشرح الآية ١٦ من سورة السجدة

﴿تَتَجَافَىٰ جُنُوبُهُمۡ عَنِ ٱلۡمَضَاجِعِ يَدۡعُونَ رَبَّهُمۡ خَوۡفٗا وَطَمَعٗا وَمِمَّا رَزَقۡنَٰهُمۡ يُنفِقُونَ﴾

“They arise from [their] beds; they supplicate their Lord in fear and aspiration, and from what We have provided them, they spend.”

💬 In plain wordsبكلامٍ بسيط

In plain words: it does not say they pray at night. It says their sides pull away from the bed — the effort is described where it is actually felt.

بعبارة أخرى: لم يقل إنهم يصلّون بالليل، وإنما إنّ جنوبهم تتجافى عن الفراش، فوُصفت المشقّة حيث تُحسّ.

The night prayer is described from the outside — what a person watching would see. Not "they pray at night", but their SIDES pull away from the beds.

The verb carries reluctance. It is the movement of something being lifted off a surface it was resting against, which is an honest description of what standing at night actually costs.

وُصف قيام الليل من خارجه، بما يراه الناظر: فلم يقل «يصلّون بالليل»، وإنما ﴿تَتَجَافَىٰ جُنُوبُهُمۡ عَنِ ٱلۡمَضَاجِعِ﴾.

وفي الفعل معنى المفارقة على ثِقَل، وهو انفكاك الشيء عن موضعٍ كان مستقرًّا عليه، وذلك وصفٌ صادقٌ لما يكلّفه القيام.

تَتَجَافَىٰtatajafa

From j-f-w, to be apart from and not settle against. Tajafi is a thing lifting away from what it was lying on.

من (ج ف و) وهو البعد وترك اللصوق. والتجافي: نبوّ الشيء عمّا كان مضطجعًا عليه.

Why this word and not anotherلِمَ هذه الكلمة دون غيرها

The subject of the verb is the SIDE, not the person. Arabic could have said they rise; it says their sides come away — which puts the effort where it is actually felt.

وفاعل الفعل الجنب لا الرجل: وقد كان يمكن أن يقال «يقومون»، فقيل تتجافى جنوبهم، فوُضعت المشقّة في موضعها الذي تُحسّ فيه.

Ar-Raghib al-Isfahani, Al-Mufradat, entry ج ف و

And no soul knows what has been hidden for them of comfort for eyes as reward for what they used to do.
Surah As-Sajdah (32:17)

Their worship is hidden in the night, and the next verse says their reward is hidden too: no soul knows what has been kept back for them.

Adh-Dhariyat describes the same people: they slept little of the night, and before dawn they were asking forgiveness.

And from [part of] the night, pray with it as additional [worship] for you; it is expected that your Lord will resurrect you to a praised station.
Surah Al-Isra (17:79)

Al-Isra gives the command to the Prophet ﷺ: and in part of the night, pray with it as an extra for you — perhaps your Lord will raise you to a praised station.

Is one who is devoutly obedient during periods of the night, prostrating and standing [in prayer], fearing the Hereafter and hoping for the mercy of his Lord, [like one who does not]? Say, "Are those who know equal to those who do not know?" Only they will remember [who are] people of understanding.
Surah Az-Zumar (39:9)

As-Sajdah gives the night prayer with fear and hope; Az-Zumar gives the same man — devout through the night, wary of the Hereafter, hoping for his Lord's mercy — and calls him one who knows.

السجدةُ تذكر قيامَ الليل بالخوف والرجاء، والزمرُ تذكر الرجلَ نفسه، وتسمّيه عالمًا: ﴿أَمَّنۡ هُوَ قَٰنِتٌ ءَانَآءَ ٱلَّيۡلِ سَاجِدٗا وَقَآئِمٗا يَحۡذَرُ ٱلۡأٓخِرَةَ وَيَرۡجُواْ رَحۡمَةَ رَبِّهِۦۗ﴾.
