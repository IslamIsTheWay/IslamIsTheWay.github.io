🧠 Tadabburتدبّر

Not just what the verse says — why it says it that way

Open any surah, press 🧠 Tadabbur beside a verse, and you are told why the verse sits where it does, why this word and not its near-synonym, where else the Quran uses it, and the verse in another surah that finishes the thought this one leaves open. Many verses now also carry why they are in that tense — why the past is used for a thing that has not happened, why the imperfect where something repeats, and what would change if it said the other. افتح أي سورة، واضغط 🧠 تدبّر بجوار الآية: لماذا جاءت الآية في موضعها، ولماذا هذه الكلمة دون قريبتها، وأين استعملها القرآن في غيرها، والآية التي تُتِمّ المعنى في سورة أخرى. وفي كثيرٍ من الآيات الآن لِمَ جاء الفعل بهذه الصيغة — لِمَ الماضي فيما لم يقع، ولِمَ المضارع حيث يتكرّر، وماذا يتغيّر لو قيل بالوجه الآخر.

A worked exampleمثال مشروح
إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ
"You alone we worship, and You alone we ask for help."

Why the object comes first. Arabic would normally say na'buduka — "we worship You". The verse puts iyyaka ("You alone") before the verb instead, and in Arabic moving a word forward is how you restrict the meaning to it. That single change is the difference between "we worship You" and "You — and no one else — do we worship." The grammar is carrying the tawhid, not just the words.

لماذا قُدِّم المفعول. كان الأصل أن يُقال «نعبدك»، فقُدِّم «إيّاك» على الفعل، وتقديم ما حقّه التأخير يفيد الحصر. فبهذا التقديم صار المعنى: لا نعبد إلا إيّاك. فالتوحيد محمولٌ في التركيب نفسه، لا في الألفاظ وحدها.

Surah Al-Fatihah (1:5) — Quran
🕰 A second example — why the tense matters مثالٌ آخر — لِمَ تهمّ الصيغة
فَإِن لَّمْ تَأْتُونِى بِهِۦ فَلَا كَيْلَ لَكُمْ عِندِى
"But if you do not bring him to me, there will be no measure for you from me." — Yusuf, 12:60
مُنِعَ مِنَّا ٱلْكَيْلُ فَأَرْسِلْ مَعَنَآ أَخَانَا
"The measure has been withheld from us, so send our brother with us." — the brothers to their father, 12:63

Three words changed, and nothing false was said. Yusuf set a condition about the future — bring him, and there is grain. His brothers reported it to their father as a completed event in the passive: it has been withheld, by nobody named. A thing Ya'qub could still have argued with became a door already shut. Every word of theirs is defensible; what was altered was the shape of the sentence.

تغيّرت ثلاث كلماتٍ ولم يُقل كذب. وضع يوسف شرطًا في المستقبل: ائتوني به تُكالوا. فنقله إخوته إلى أبيهم ماضيًا مبنيًّا للمجهول: قد مُنِع، ولم يُسمَّ من منعه. فصار ما كان ليعقوب أن يُناقشه بابًا مُغلقًا. وكلّ لفظٍ من ألفاظهم له وجه، وإنما تغيّرت صورة الجملة.

Common Questions


What can I find in the Quran Library?

All 114 surahs with their Arabic names, meanings, number of verses, and whether they were revealed in Makkah or Madinah. Open any surah to read it in Arabic with an English translation, choose from 16 reciters, and follow along by Mushaf page.

How do I know a hadith here is authentic?

Every hadith on this site carries its grading and names the scholar behind it — "Sahih — Narrated by al-Bukhari", "Sahih — Agreed upon". Anything outside Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim is labelled as such, and material taken from the historical sira is marked as history rather than hadith. Where something well known is not authentic, the site says so on the entry itself.

What is on the Stories page?

52 moments from the life of the Prophet ﷺ. Each was located in the text of Sahih al-Bukhari or Sahih Muslim before it was written here, and each carries the Arabic he spoke, the reference, the grading, and what the situation teaches — in both languages.

What does the Day of Judgement page cover?

Fifteen stages in the order they occur, from death to the gates, built on 54 passages of the Quran and hadith from the two Sahihs. Where a widely repeated detail sits outside them — the names of the angels of the grave, for instance — the page states that plainly instead of presenting it as settled.

How do I find something for my own situation?

On the Guidance page, describe what you are going through by typing or speaking, in Arabic or English. The site searches the Quranic themes, the Sunnah, the worship steps, the full Bukhari and Muslim collections, and the lives of the Prophets and Companions, and shows what relates to it with full references. It is not a fatwa — read each verse in its full surah and consult a qualified scholar.

Is the content free to access?

Yes. All content on IslamBasics is free, intended for learning, reflection, and sharing knowledge of Islam with others.
